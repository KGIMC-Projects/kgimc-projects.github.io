Healing & Restoration

The Fragrance of Her Crushing: When God Brings Beauty Out of Brokenness

The crushing does not give you your identity. Jesus does.
By Anita Singh

There are seasons in our walk with God when we face pain, rejection, betrayal, and disappointment. Sometimes the very situations we prayed would change become the places where God does His deepest work within us. God is not the author of every painful experience, but He can use what we have endured to refine our character, deepen our faith, and bring beauty out of our brokenness.

The Crushing Can Produce Something Precious

In biblical times, precious oils and fragrances were released through pressing or crushing. This gives us a powerful picture of how God can bring something valuable out of difficult seasons. Paul writes in 2 Corinthians 4:8–9 that we may be troubled, perplexed, persecuted, and struck down—yet not destroyed. Sometimes we ask God to remove the pressure while He is teaching us to depend on His strength in the middle of it. The purpose is not suffering itself, but what God accomplishes through our surrender.

The Alabaster Jar Must Be Broken

In Mark 14:3–9, a woman came to Jesus with an alabaster flask of costly perfume. She broke the flask and poured it over His head. Others criticized her, but Jesus defended her act of worship. The perfume was valuable, yet she considered Jesus worthy of the sacrifice. True surrender means giving Jesus more than what is convenient. It means laying down pride, fear, control, and even our personal plans. The fragrance of worship is released through a heart fully yielded to Him.

God Works in the Secret Place

Not every season of preparation is visible. David was shaped in the fields long before he was crowned king. Joseph remained faithful through betrayal, slavery, and imprisonment before stepping into leadership. Their hidden seasons were not wasted. Sometimes God is developing patience, humility, wisdom, discernment, forgiveness, and endurance before entrusting us with greater responsibility. God cares not only about what we accomplish publicly but who we become privately.

Your Pain Does Not Define Your Identity

Rejection can make us feel unwanted. Betrayal can make us guarded. Disappointment can make us question our worth. But our identity must be rooted in what God says—not in what others have done. Song of Solomon 2:10 shows the beloved calling his loved one to arise and come away. This tender picture reminds us of the importance of drawing near to God. You may have been wounded, but you do not have to remain imprisoned by those wounds. Through Christ, healing and restoration are possible.

The Fragrance of Christ Revealed Through Us

Second Corinthians 2:14–15 describes believers as the fragrance of Christ. When someone walks through difficult seasons and still demonstrates love, humility, forgiveness, faithfulness, and compassion, others witness God’s transforming work. The evidence of spiritual maturity is not how much suffering we have endured, but how much we are becoming like Jesus.

Do Not Confuse Crushing With Destruction

Not every painful situation is a divine assignment, and not every hardship comes from God. Sometimes He is leading us out of harmful circumstances—not asking us to remain in them. God can redeem suffering without being the One who caused it. Romans 8:28 reminds us that God works all things together for good for those who love Him and are called according to His purpose. This does not mean everything that happens is good—but nothing is beyond His ability to redeem.

When the Season Changes

There comes a time when what God developed in private begins to influence how we live publicly. What once produced bitterness can become a testimony of forgiveness. What once produced fear can become an opportunity to exercise faith. What once overwhelmed us no longer controls our responses. Isaiah 61:3 speaks of beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, and the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness. God can bring beauty out of what appeared ruined.

Final Encouragement

If you are walking through a difficult season, do not assume God has abandoned you. Bring your pain before Him. Allow His Word to renew your mind, His love to heal your heart, and His wisdom to guide your decisions. Do not become so focused on the crushing that you lose sight of the One who brings restoration.

The greatest fragrance we carry is not the evidence of how much we have suffered, but the evidence of how deeply Christ has transformed our lives.

The crushing does not give you your identity. Jesus does. And even the places where you have been broken can become places where His grace is revealed.

— Anita Singh
