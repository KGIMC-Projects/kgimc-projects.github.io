The Difference Between Being Gifted and Being Spiritually Mature

By Anita Singh

One of the greatest misunderstandings in the Body of Christ is assuming that because someone is spiritually gifted, they are also spiritually mature. A person may preach powerfully, prophesy, teach God's Word, or operate in spiritual gifts, yet still struggle with pride, jealousy, anger, unforgiveness, and a lack of self-control.

Being gifted is not the same as being spiritually mature. Your gift may open doors, but without the character, wisdom, and maturity to handle what comes with those opportunities, your success can become part of your downfall.

God is not only concerned about developing our gifts. He is concerned about developing the person carrying them.

Your Gift May Open the Door, but Can Your Character Sustain You?

Proverbs 18:16 (NKJV) says, "A man's gift makes room for him, And brings him before great men."

Although this proverb speaks of a gift or present opening access to influential people, it illustrates an important principle about opportunity: gaining access is not the same as being prepared for responsibility.

We see this in Hollywood and the entertainment industry. Someone may possess extraordinary talent and become famous almost overnight. Suddenly, they have wealth, recognition, power, and influence, but may not have developed the discipline, wisdom, and emotional maturity necessary to handle it. Some become overwhelmed and make destructive decisions involving substance abuse, unhealthy relationships, pride, or reckless living.

The same principle applies in ministry. Someone may be highly gifted and influential but lack the humility, integrity, self-control, and accountability needed to steward that influence.

Your gift may take you to the mountaintop, but your character will help determine how faithfully you stand there.

Spiritual Gifts Are Not the Same as Spiritual Fruit

In 1 Corinthians 12:4–11, Paul explains that the Holy Spirit distributes spiritual gifts for the benefit of the Body of Christ. Galatians 5:22–23 describes the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

Spiritual gifts demonstrate what the Holy Spirit can accomplish through us, while spiritual fruit reveals the transformation He is producing within us.

The Corinthian church was not lacking in spiritual gifts (1 Corinthians 1:7), yet Paul confronted its jealousy, strife, divisions, and immaturity (1 Corinthians 3:1–3).

Being used by God does not mean every area of our character has been surrendered to Him.

Samson: When Gifting Is Not Supported by Character

Samson was chosen by God and empowered with extraordinary strength to deliver Israel from the Philistines (Judges 13–16). Yet despite his calling, he repeatedly demonstrated poor judgment, disobedience, and a lack of self-control. His destructive choices brought serious consequences.

Proverbs 16:18 (NKJV) warns, "Pride goes before destruction, And a haughty spirit before a fall."

The anointing on your life does not excuse the areas of your character God is calling you to surrender.

Joseph and David: Character Developed Through the Process

Joseph received dreams concerning his future, but he did not move directly from the promise to the palace. He experienced betrayal, slavery, false accusation, and imprisonment (Genesis 37–41). Through these seasons, he demonstrated integrity, faithfulness, patience, and dependence on God. When Potiphar's wife attempted to seduce him, Joseph refused to compromise, even at a personal cost (Genesis 39:7–12).

David was anointed by Samuel in 1 Samuel 16, but he did not immediately become king. He faced years of opposition and uncertainty. Even when he had opportunities to kill Saul, he refused to take the throne through wrongdoing (1 Samuel 24; 26).

Receiving a promise or anointing is not the same as being prepared to carry the full responsibility of an assignment.

Don't Rush the Process: God Is Developing What You Will Need

One of the greatest mistakes we can make is wanting to reach the place God has called us without going through the process necessary to prepare us. We want promotion, open doors, influence, and blessings, but we do not always welcome the waiting, testing, correction, and challenges that develop our character.

Romans 5:3–4 teaches that tribulation produces perseverance, perseverance develops character, and character produces hope. The process can develop endurance when we feel like giving up, humility when we receive correction, and faithfulness when nobody is watching.

Not every hardship is sent by God, but He can work through our experiences to deepen our faith and develop our character.

James 1:4 (NKJV) says, "But let patience have its perfect work, that you may be perfect and complete, lacking nothing."

When we become impatient, we may try to force doors open or look for shortcuts. But a shortcut may get us somewhere faster without developing what we need to remain faithful when we arrive.

Don't despise the process. The preparation you are tempted to rush may be developing the very character you will need to sustain you where God is taking you.

When Your Gift Becomes Greater Than Your Character

Recognition and influence can expose weaknesses that were easier to overlook when nobody was watching. Pride, selfish ambition, unhealthy desires, and resistance to correction can become destructive when combined with power.

Jesus warned in Matthew 7:22–23 that outward ministry activities are not proof of a genuine relationship with Him. He also declared in Matthew 7:20 (NKJV), "Therefore by their fruits you will know them."

In 1 Corinthians 13:1–3, Paul teaches that even extraordinary spiritual gifts are nothing without love.

The same Holy Spirit who empowers us to minister also desires to transform how we live.

How Do We Develop the Character to Carry Our Gifts?

Remain humble and teachable. Never become too successful to receive correction (Proverbs 12:1).

Allow God to examine your heart. Ask Him to reveal pride, jealousy, bitterness, and hidden motives (Psalm 139:23–24).

Develop self-control. Govern your words, emotions, decisions, and relationships (Galatians 5:22–23).

Welcome godly accountability. Surround yourself with people who will speak truth, not simply celebrate your gifts (Proverbs 27:17).

Choose obedience over recognition. Never compromise your integrity for promotion or influence (James 1:22).

Remain faithful through preparation. Allow perseverance to develop maturity rather than looking for shortcuts (James 1:2–4).

Keep your relationship with God above your ministry. Never become so busy working for God that you neglect walking with Him (John 15:4–5).

Final Reflection: Let God Develop the Person Carrying the Gift

God has entrusted His people with spiritual gifts, talents, abilities, and opportunities for His purposes. Whether someone is called to ministry, business, leadership, education, entertainment, or another sphere of influence, character matters.

Samson reminds us of the danger of gifting without self-control. Joseph and David demonstrate the value of integrity, patience, and faithfulness through preparation. The Corinthian church teaches us that spiritual activity does not automatically equal spiritual maturity.

Spiritual maturity does not mean perfection. It means remaining humble, teachable, accountable, and willing to repent and grow.

Do not despise preparation, rush ahead of God, or look for shortcuts. Sometimes the situations you want God to remove are the very situations He can use to develop the endurance and wisdom you will need for the responsibilities ahead.

It is not enough to have the gift to reach the mountaintop. You must also develop the character to remain faithful when you get there.

Let God develop both your gift and the person carrying it.

A Question to Consider

Are you spending more time developing your gifts and pursuing opportunities than allowing God to develop the character necessary to steward them?

Prayer

Heavenly Father, thank You for the gifts, talents, and abilities You have entrusted to us. Help us never to become so focused on recognition, promotion, or influence that we neglect our relationship with You. Develop in us humility, integrity, wisdom, endurance, perseverance, discipline, and spiritual maturity. Teach us not to rush the process or look for shortcuts, but to trust You through every season of preparation. Search our hearts, correct what needs to change, and help us reflect the character of Jesus Christ in both our public and private lives. May our gifts always bring glory to You. In Jesus' name, Amen.

— Anita Singh
