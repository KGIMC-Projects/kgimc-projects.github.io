When Helping Someone Becomes Carrying What God Never Asked You to Carry

By Anita Singh

Having a heart to help others is a beautiful quality. God calls us to love, encourage, pray for, and support one another. But sometimes, without realizing it, we move from helping people to carrying responsibilities that were never ours to carry.

We become consumed with their problems, constantly trying to fix situations, make decisions for them, or prevent them from experiencing consequences. Before long, we may find ourselves emotionally exhausted, spiritually drained, and neglecting responsibilities God has entrusted to us.

The question is not whether we should help people. Scripture clearly teaches us to do so. The question is whether we are offering healthy support or taking responsibility for choices and obligations that belong to someone else.

God calls us to help carry one another's burdens, but He never intended us to become responsible for every decision, problem, and outcome in another person's life.

There Is a Difference Between Helping and Carrying

Galatians 6:2 (NKJV) says, "Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ."

Yet Galatians 6:5 also says, "For each one shall bear his own load."

These verses are not contradictory. They teach different aspects of Christian responsibility. We are called to support one another through genuine difficulties while recognizing that each person also has responsibilities before God.

Helping someone may involve prayer, encouragement, practical assistance, financial generosity when appropriate, or walking alongside them through a difficult season. But continually making their decisions, managing responsibilities they can handle, or shielding them from accountability can become unhealthy.

Compassion helps people carry what is difficult. Unhealthy responsibility tries to carry what belongs to them.

Moses and Jethro: Even Godly Leaders Cannot Carry Everything

In Exodus 18:13–24, Moses spent his days judging disputes among the people. From morning until evening, he carried an overwhelming responsibility.

When his father-in-law Jethro observed what was happening, he recognized that Moses could not continue handling everything alone.

Exodus 18:18 (NKJV) says, "Both you and these people who are with you will surely wear yourselves out. For this thing is too much for you; you are not able to perform it by yourself."

Jethro advised Moses to appoint capable, trustworthy men to share the responsibility. Moses listened and established a healthier structure of leadership.

Moses was carrying a legitimate assignment from God, but he needed wisdom about how that assignment should be fulfilled. God can provide people, structures, and practical solutions so we do not carry everything alone.

Just because you are capable of helping does not mean you are supposed to do everything yourself.

Martha and Mary: When Serving Distracts Us From What Matters Most

In Luke 10:38–42, Martha welcomed Jesus into her home and became occupied with preparing and serving. Her sister Mary sat at Jesus' feet and listened to His teaching.

Martha became frustrated and asked Jesus to tell Mary to help her. Jesus gently responded that Martha was worried and troubled about many things, while Mary had chosen what was needed most.

Jesus was not condemning hospitality or practical service. He was addressing Martha's anxiety and misplaced priorities.

Sometimes we become so busy meeting everyone else's needs that we neglect our relationship with God, our families, our health, or the responsibilities already entrusted to us.

Being constantly busy helping others does not automatically mean we are giving our attention to what God considers most important.

Nehemiah: Knowing When Not to Leave Your Assignment

In Nehemiah 6:1–4, Nehemiah was rebuilding Jerusalem's wall when Sanballat and Geshem repeatedly invited him to meet with them. Nehemiah recognized that their intentions were harmful and refused to abandon his work.

Nehemiah 6:3 (NKJV) says, "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down."

Nehemiah understood that responding to every demand could distract him from the assignment he had been entrusted to complete.

— Anita Singh
