When Closure Never Comes: How to Heal Anyway

Peace Does Not Require Another Person's Permission
By Anita Singh

"He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds." — Psalm 147:3 NKJV

One of the hardest things to accept is that sometimes closure never comes. You may never receive the apology. You may never get the explanation. You may never fully understand why someone changed, betrayed you, disappeared, lied, withdrew, or chose another path.

If you are not careful, you can spend years waiting for another person to give you the peace only God can give.

Sometimes we tell ourselves, “If they would just explain, then I could move on.” But what happens when they never explain? What happens when the conversation never comes? What happens when the person refuses accountability or simply walks away?

Healing cannot remain dependent on another person’s willingness to participate.

Ecclesiastes 3:6 says there is “a time to keep, and a time to throw away.” — NKJV

Sometimes closure is not a conversation. Sometimes closure is accepting what their actions already revealed. You may not know every reason, but you can still recognize the fruit. You may not know why they changed, but you can acknowledge what the relationship became.

There is a difference between wanting understanding and needing someone else to validate your reality. You know what you experienced. God knows what happened. You do not have to keep reopening the wound hoping someone else will finally explain it correctly.

Sometimes the need for closure is really the need for justice, validation, reassurance, or confirmation that what happened mattered. Those needs are real, but there comes a point when healing requires surrendering the unanswered questions.

Deuteronomy 29:29 says, “The secret things belong to the Lord our God.” — NKJV

There are things you may never know, and peace can still come.

Forgiveness is one of the greatest keys to closure because forgiveness helps set you free from the control of what happened.

Forgiving someone does not mean they deserved it. It does not mean what they did was acceptable. It does not mean the pain did not matter. Forgiveness means you are making a decision that what they did will no longer have permission to keep ruling your heart.

Sometimes the person who hurt you has already moved on. They may be living their life, laughing, building, succeeding, and hardly thinking about what happened. Meanwhile, you can spend years carrying bitterness, anger, pain, disappointment, and unforgiveness.

That is too expensive.

Unforgiveness can keep you emotionally tied to people you say you want freedom from. Every time you replay what they did, rehearse what you wish you had said, imagine what they should suffer, or wait for an apology that never comes, part of your heart remains connected to the offense.

Ephesians 4:31—32 says, “Let all bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, and evil speaking be put away from you, with all malice. And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you.” — NKJV

Sometimes they will never apologize. Sometimes they will never admit what they did. Sometimes they will never understand how deeply they hurt you.

You still have a decision to make: Will I continue carrying this, or will I release it?

Forgiveness is not giving them permission to hurt you again. You can forgive and keep the boundary. You can forgive and never return to the same relationship. You can forgive and still require accountability.

Forgiveness is about setting your heart free.

We can hold on until bitterness begins eating away at our peace, joy, ability to trust, and even the good things God is trying to build in our lives—or we can place the offense into God’s hands and say, “You may never say sorry, but I will not remain imprisoned by what you did.”

Sometimes closure begins right there. Not when they apologize. Not when they explain. Not when they finally understand. But when you decide, “I forgive you, I release you, and I choose to move forward.”

Philippians 3:13—14 says, “Forgetting those things which are behind and reaching forward to those things which are ahead, I press toward the goal.” — NKJV

You do not have to erase your memory to move forward. You simply stop allowing the memory to govern your direction.

Declaration, Binding & Loosing

I declare that my healing will not depend on another person giving me closure.

According to Matthew 18:18, I loose out of my soul all bitterness, unforgiveness, resentment, anger, revenge, obsession with unanswered questions, emotional attachment to the past, false hope, confusion, regret, self-blame, rejection, disappointment, and every need to receive validation from the person who wounded me, in Jesus’ name.

I bind to my soul forgiveness, peace, acceptance, clarity, wisdom, emotional freedom, healthy detachment, courage, hope, renewed focus, healthy boundaries, and the ability to move forward without every answer, in Jesus’ name.

I release the conversations that never happened, the apologies I never received, and the explanations that may never come. I do not need another person’s permission to heal.

Healing Prayer

Father, in the name of Jesus, I bring You every unanswered question, unfinished conversation, broken promise, unexplained withdrawal, betrayal, and disappointment that has kept part of my heart attached to the past.

Heal the part of me that keeps waiting for answers. Heal the part of me that still hopes another person will return and finally make everything make sense.

Where I have blamed myself for things I could not control, free me. Where I have replayed the past searching for answers, quiet my mind. Where I have needed another person to validate my pain, remind me that You saw everything.

Help me forgive even when no apology comes. Help me release the offense even when the other person never admits what they did. Remove bitterness, resentment, anger, revenge, and every emotional attachment that keeps me connected to the pain.

I choose to forgive, not because what happened was right, but because I want to be free.

Help me accept what I cannot change and release what I cannot understand. Give me peace without explanation, healing without an apology, and freedom without another conversation.

I surrender every unanswered question to You. I choose peace over obsession, truth over fantasy, forgiveness over bitterness, and healing over emotional captivity.

What ended will not imprison what You still have ahead for me. In Jesus’ name, amen.

— Anita Singh
