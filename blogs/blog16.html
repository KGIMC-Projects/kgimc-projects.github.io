Obedience When You Do Not Understand the Assignment

Sometimes before looking for the next step, we need to obey the last one.
By Anita Singh

Walking with God often means obeying before understanding. As human beings, we want the full blueprint, the timeline, the destination, and an explanation of what will happen when we get there. But God does not always work that way. Sometimes He gives enough direction for the next step and allows the rest of the assignment to unfold as we obey.

God may tell you to go somewhere without explaining everything He intends to do when you arrive. He may tell you to prepare without showing you what you are preparing for. He may tell you to release something before showing you what comes next. In those moments, obedience reveals whether we trust God enough to move without having everything figured out first.

Abraham is a powerful example. Hebrews 11:8 NKJV says, “By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to the place which he would receive as an inheritance. And he went out, not knowing where he was going.” God did not hand Abraham the entire blueprint before he moved. Abraham had enough direction to obey, and further understanding came along the journey.

Sometimes we say, “Lord, show me everything, and then I will go.” But God may be calling us to obey what He has already made clear and trust Him to reveal what we need along the way. Faith does not require us to know the entire journey before taking the first obedient step.

Noah lived the same pattern. God told him to build an ark because a flood was coming—something Noah had never experienced. The assignment was extraordinary, but Noah followed the instructions God gave him. Genesis 6:22 NKJV says, “Thus Noah did; according to all that God commanded him, so he did.” Noah did not need to experience the flood first before believing God enough to prepare for it. He built before he saw what God had warned him was coming. That is obedience: trusting God enough to act on His instruction even when you have never seen anything like it before.

Peter learned a similar lesson on the Sea of Galilee. After a night of failure, Jesus told him to let down the nets again. Peter responded, “Nevertheless at Your word I will let down the net” (Luke 5:5 NKJV). His experience said one thing, but the word of Jesus required another response.

When Obedience Challenges Your Expectations

Sometimes God’s direction will stretch you, interrupt your plans, or confront your assumptions. You may not understand why He asks you to wait, go, release something, forgive, prepare, begin again, or walk a different path than the one you envisioned. Understanding may come later, or you may never receive every explanation you wanted.

God already knows our hearts, but testing can reveal what is in us, strengthen our faith, and teach us obedience. Deuteronomy 8:2 speaks of God testing Israel in the wilderness “to know what was in your heart, whether you would keep His commandments or not.”

There is also a crucial difference between not understanding God’s instruction and not knowing whether the instruction came from God.

Discernment Before Obedience

Faith does not require you to treat every thought, feeling, dream, prophecy, or instruction from another person as the voice of God. First Thessalonians 5:21 NKJV says, “Test all things; hold fast what is good.”

What you believe you are hearing should be tested against Scripture, through prayer for wisdom, with godly motives, and by spiritual discernment rather than impulse. God will never ask you to violate His written Word in order to obey Him.

This also applies when someone says, “God told me you need to do this.” Spiritual authority is never a tool for manipulation. No pastor, prophet, leader, friend, or family member replaces your responsibility to follow Christ and remain grounded in Scripture.

Go Back to the Last Thing God Made Clear

There are seasons when we say, “God, I cannot hear You. I do not know what to do next.” Before assuming God has stopped leading, it can be wise to examine our previous obedience.

Is there something God already made clear that you neglected? Is there an apology you still need to make, forgiveness you have refused to extend, something you were supposed to prepare, a habit you were supposed to leave, or a simple act of obedience you dismissed because it seemed too small?

This does not mean every time God seems quiet you have been disobedient. Scripture shows faithful people who experienced seasons of waiting. But sometimes we are asking God for the next assignment while ignoring something He already placed in front of us.

Jesus said in Luke 16:10 NKJV, “He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much.” Do not despise the little acts of obedience because you are waiting for a greater assignment. What seems small to you may be part of your preparation.

Sometimes we want a new word when we need to return to the last thing God already made clear. Go back. Pray honestly. Ask the Holy Spirit to search your heart. If there is unfinished obedience, repent where necessary and do what you know you should have done.

The next assignment may require faithfulness in the previous one.

When God Has Already Made the Next Step Clear

Once God’s direction has been discerned, endless questioning can become delayed obedience. Sometimes we keep asking for the whole blueprint when God has already given us the next step. We want to know where it will lead, how it will work, who will help us, and what will happen afterward. But sometimes those details become clearer as we walk in obedience.

Proverbs 3:5–6 NKJV says, “Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways acknowledge Him, and He shall direct your paths.”

This does not tell us to stop thinking or seeking wisdom. It tells us not to make our limited understanding the foundation of our trust.

You do not need to know everything God knows in order to obey what He has made clear. Stay grounded in Scripture. Test what you believe you are hearing. Pray for wisdom. Reject manipulation. Examine whether there is unfinished obedience from a previous season. Then, when God’s direction has been clearly discerned, obey even without the full blueprint.

Sometimes God gives you the next step before He gives you the explanation. And sometimes before looking for the next step, we need to obey the last one.

Prayer

Father, teach me to obey You even when You have not shown me the entire blueprint. Search my heart and show me if there is anything You have already made clear that I have neglected or refused to do. Forgive me for delayed or partial obedience. Help me to be faithful even in the things that seem small. Give me discernment to recognize Your direction, wisdom to test what I believe I am hearing, and faith to take the next step without demanding to see the entire journey. In Jesus’ name, amen.

— Anita Singh
