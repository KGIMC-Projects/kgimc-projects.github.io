When Your Heart Keeps Choosing Unavailable People

Why the Heart Sometimes Returns to What Cannot Fully Choose It
By Anita Singh

"Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life." — Proverbs 4:23 NKJV

Sometimes the problem is not that we do not know what healthy love looks like. Sometimes unhealthy love simply feels more familiar. A person can genuinely desire peace, commitment, honesty, and stability while repeatedly becoming attached to people who are emotionally, spiritually, relationally, or practically unavailable.

The pattern may look different each time, but the feeling is familiar: waiting, hoping, interpreting mixed signals, making excuses, accepting partial access, or believing that if you love enough, pray enough, or remain patient enough, eventually the person will become available. But love cannot make an unavailable person available.

Sometimes the heart is drawn toward what feels familiar rather than what is healthy. If you learned early in life that love had to be chased, earned, waited for, or fought for, consistency may feel unfamiliar while uncertainty feels strangely normal.

An unavailable person may be married, emotionally closed, inconsistent, unwilling to commit, spiritually incompatible, still attached to a former relationship, or simply unable to offer the kind of relationship you desire. Strong chemistry does not change those realities. A person can care about you and still be unavailable.

Amos 3:3 says, “Can two walk together, unless they are agreed?”

Healthy relationships require more than attraction. They require availability, shared values, honesty, responsibility, consistency, and a willingness to move in the same direction.

Imagine someone who repeatedly finds herself attracted to people who cannot fully choose her. Every relationship begins with intensity, deep conversation, affection, attention, and hope. Eventually she finds herself waiting again—waiting for clarity, commitment, circumstances to change, or the person to become ready.

After enough time, she may begin believing that waiting is what love feels like. But there is a difference between patience and postponing your life. There is a difference between giving someone time and building your future around their indecision. There is a difference between compassion and becoming emotionally trapped in someone else’s unresolved situation.

Sometimes healing requires asking, “Why does my heart feel safest pursuing people who cannot fully choose me?”

The answer may involve abandonment, rejection, low self-worth, fear of intimacy, childhood patterns, previous betrayal, or the belief that love must be difficult to be valuable.

Psalm 139:23—24 says, “Search me, O God, and know my heart; try me, and know my anxieties; and see if there is any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.”

God can reveal patterns without condemning you. Healing is not about blaming yourself for everyone you have loved. It is about becoming aware of what your heart has learned to accept.

Sometimes we enter or remain in unavailable relationships because there is a need, desire, longing, or empty place in us that has not yet been fully surrendered to God. It may be a longing to be chosen, seen, loved, protected, validated, rescued, or finally given what we felt was missing before.

Those desires are not automatically wrong. But anything we refuse to surrender can become a vulnerable place.

James 1:14 says, “But each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own desires and enticed.”

This is why surrender matters. The enemy often tries to exploit the places where we are hungry, wounded, impatient, lonely, or unwilling to trust God. A legitimate desire can become dangerous when we allow it to lead us outside wisdom, truth, or obedience.

Sometimes the question is not only, “Why is this person unavailable?” but also, “What is this relationship feeding in me that I have not yet fully surrendered to God?”

Is it feeding loneliness? The need to be chosen? The fear of being alone? The desire to rescue? The need for attention? The hope that someone else will heal what only God can heal?

Sometimes we can also tell ourselves, “I don’t want a relationship. I chose singleness. I’m fine by myself.” And perhaps for a season, we truly believe it.

Then an old friend, a former flame, or someone we once cared about suddenly reappears. They invite us out “just as friends.” We spend time together, talk, laugh, reconnect, and something inside begins to awaken.

Suddenly we realize, “Maybe I have not been completely honest with myself.”

Perhaps underneath the independence there is still a genuine desire for companionship, romance, partnership, affection, intimacy, and to share life with another person.

There is nothing shameful about recognizing that desire.

Genesis 2:18 says, “It is not good that man should be alone; I will make him a helper comparable to him.” — NKJV

The problem is not necessarily the desire for companionship. The question is what we do with that desire once we recognize it.

Sometimes we call ourselves content when we are actually afraid to hope again. Sometimes “I choose singleness” can really mean, “I do not want to be hurt again.” Sometimes independence becomes armor after rejection, betrayal, disappointment, or relationships that required too much emotional survival.

Then someone awakens a part of the heart we thought was gone.

That moment can reveal something important: the desire was not dead; it had simply been buried.

And that is where honesty with God becomes necessary.

Instead of denying the longing, we can bring it before Him and say, “Lord, I do desire companionship. I do desire healthy romantic love. But I surrender that desire to You. Do not allow my hunger for relationship to make me accept what is unavailable, unhealthy, or outside Your wisdom.”

Psalm 37:4 says, “Delight yourself also in the Lord, and He shall give you the desires of your heart.” — NKJV

Surrender does not require us to pretend we have no desires. It means we refuse to let those desires become our master.

You can desire marriage and still refuse the wrong relationship. You can desire companionship and still maintain boundaries. You can admit that you want romantic love without chasing someone who cannot fully offer it.

Sometimes healing begins with simply telling God the truth: “Lord, I thought I no longer wanted this, but I do. Now teach me how to carry this desire without allowing it to carry me.”

The things we refuse to face are often the very things the enemy tries to use to trap us, tempt us, hurt us, and distract us. One of the greatest lessons I have learned is that these are the areas where we must become honest with ourselves and honest before God.

We cannot heal what we keep denying. We cannot surrender what we refuse to acknowledge. We cannot ask God to restore an area we keep hiding from Him.

Sometimes the breakthrough begins when we finally say, “Lord, this is what I really feel. This is what I really want. This is what still hurts. This is where I am still vulnerable.”

That kind of honesty is not weakness. It is the beginning of healing.

Psalm 51:6 says, “Behold, You desire truth in the inward parts, and in the hidden part You will make me to know wisdom.” — NKJV

The places we avoid can become places of vulnerability, but the places we surrender can become places of healing, wisdom, freedom, and strength. This is why we must allow the Lord to search us, expose what is hidden, heal what is wounded, and teach us how to walk in truth.

You do not have to keep proving your worth to unavailable people. You do not have to compete with another relationship, another priority, another unresolved situation, or someone else’s indecision. You do not have to keep waiting for potential to become reality.

Love should not require you to disappear inside someone else’s confusion.

Declaration, Binding & Loosing

I declare that I will no longer build my life around unavailable relationships. According to Matthew 18:18, I loose out of my soul all fear of loneliness, rejection, emotional dependency, unhealthy attachment, fantasy, false hope, obsession, confusion, desperation, unsurrendered desires, and every pattern that draws me toward people who cannot fully choose me, in Jesus’ name.

I bind to my soul clarity, wisdom, discernment, patience, self-respect, courage, peace, emotional stability, healthy boundaries, secure identity, surrender to God, and the ability to recognize relationships that are available, honest, mutual, and aligned with God, in Jesus’ name.

I declare that I do not need to chase love, compete for love, or convince anyone to choose me. I surrender every desire, longing, expectation, and unmet need to the Lord. I will not confuse inconsistency with passion, waiting with commitment, or potential with reality. My heart belongs to God before it belongs to anyone else.

Healing Prayer

Father, in the name of Jesus, search my heart and reveal every wound, belief, unmet need, desire, longing, or pattern that has caused me to repeatedly become attached to unavailable people.

Heal rejection, abandonment, loneliness, low self-worth, fear of intimacy, and every place where I learned that love must be chased, earned, or waited for.

I surrender to You my desire to be loved, chosen, seen, understood, protected, valued, and wanted. I surrender every longing I have tried to satisfy outside of Your wisdom and timing.

Where I have confused chemistry with compatibility, give me wisdom. Where I have mistaken attention for commitment, give me clarity. Where I have become emotionally attached to potential instead of reality, help me release what is not available to me.

Where I have hidden behind singleness because I was afraid to admit that I still desire companionship, bring me into honesty with You. Teach me to acknowledge my desires without allowing them to control my decisions.

Close every vulnerable place in my heart that temptation has been able to exploit. Teach me not to allow loneliness, longing, impatience, emotional hunger, or unsurrendered desires to lead me into compromise.

Give me courage to face what I have avoided, truth to acknowledge what is really in my heart, and grace to surrender every wounded place to You.

Teach me to stop building my future around another person’s indecision. Give me peace when I need to walk away, patience when I need to wait on You, and discernment to recognize healthy love when it appears.

Heal my heart until consistency no longer feels boring, peace no longer feels unfamiliar, and healthy love no longer feels suspicious. I release every unavailable relationship, false hope, fantasy, unhealthy attachment, and unsurrendered desire that keeps me from moving forward.

Restore my expectations, boundaries, confidence, and trust in You. Lead me into relationships marked by truth, mutuality, availability, respect, spiritual alignment, and peace. In Jesus’ name, amen.

— Anita Singh
